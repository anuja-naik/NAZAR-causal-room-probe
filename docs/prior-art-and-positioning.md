# Prior-art posture and novelty boundaries

## What the supplied concept says

The supplied NAZAR brief acknowledges that individual elements have related prior work: optical lens-reflection detection, wireless traffic analysis, light-stimulus camera detection, and RF localization. It names Blink/Flicker and MotionCompass as examples, but provides no full bibliographic references or exact claim-by-claim comparison.

## Repository position

NAZAR is positioned as an **integration and workflow contribution**, not as a claim that the team invented hidden-camera detection or any individual sensing technique. The proposed combination is:

- a fresh phone-known light challenge, measured rather than assumed;
- a causal-response test on encrypted wireless metadata, with lag, surrogate comparison, multiple-flow correction, and a second challenge;
- a covered-lens null control;
- rough RF regionalization followed by independent optical confirmation;
- conservative evidence tiers/coverage and a tamper-evident report workflow.

This is a project positioning statement, not a patentability, novelty, or freedom-to-operate opinion. The repository does not claim experimentally demonstrated superiority over other systems.

## Citation gap / pre-event research item

The supplied source does not include titles, authors, venues, dates, or URLs for the named examples. No bibliography is fabricated here. Before making comparative claims, the team should locate and verify the exact primary sources, record what each work measures and assumes, and update this file with citations and a careful comparison. Until then, restrict claims to the supplied concept’s own framing and label the literature review incomplete.

## Safe wording

Prefer:

> “NAZAR combines a secret light challenge, causal analysis of encrypted traffic metadata, independent optical confirmation, rough regionalization, and verifiable evidence in a phone-first workflow.”

Avoid:

- “We invented hidden-camera detection.”
- “No existing system does this.”
- “The first / only / guaranteed detector.”
- Any comparison of accuracy, speed, or reliability without cited and reproducible measurements.
